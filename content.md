## A concerted [4+2]

The **Diels–Alder reaction** joins a conjugated **diene** and an alkene **dienophile** into a six-membered ring, making **two new carbon–carbon bonds at once**. It is a *pericyclic* reaction: every bond breaks and forms together in a single cyclic transition state — there are no intermediates. Here the diene is **cyclopentadiene** and the dienophile is **maleic anhydride**, and the product is the bicyclic **endo** adduct, *cis-5-norbornene-endo-2,3-dicarboxylic anhydride*.

Two features make this pairing react fast, even in an ice bath: cyclopentadiene is permanently locked in the reactive **s-cis** shape, and the two carbonyls of maleic anhydride make it an especially **electron-poor** dienophile.

## 2 · Mechanism & pathway

The reaction is a **concerted [4+2] cycloaddition**: the four π electrons of the diene and the two π electrons of the dienophile flow around one cyclic transition state.

<div data-mount="pathway"></div>

<div class="mech-grid">
<article class="mech-step"><span class="step-num">1</span>
<h3>The diene turns s-cis</h3>
<p>The diene must be <strong><em>s-cis</em></strong> so its two double bonds lie coplanar and on the same side. <strong>Cyclopentadiene</strong> is locked that way by its ring — which is why it reacts so readily (and slowly dimerizes on standing).</p>
</article>
<article class="mech-step major-accent"><span class="step-num">2</span>
<h3>Two bonds form at once</h3>
<p>In a single step the diene termini (C1 and C4) bond to the two dienophile carbons. <strong>Two new σ C–C bonds</strong> and a new six-membered ring form together; the addition is <strong>suprafacial on both partners</strong>, so it is stereospecific <em>syn</em> addition.</p>
</article>
<article class="mech-step minor-accent"><span class="step-num">3</span>
<h3>Endo wins (kinetics)</h3>
<p>Two transition states are possible. The <strong>endo</strong> one is favored by <em>secondary orbital interactions</em> between the diene and the anhydride carbonyls, so the endo adduct is the <strong>kinetic major product</strong> (exo is the minor one).</p>
</article>
</div>

<div class="callout info">
<h3>Frontier orbitals — why it's fast</h3>
<p>The dominant interaction is the diene <strong>HOMO</strong> with the dienophile <strong>LUMO</strong>. The electron-withdrawing carbonyls of maleic anhydride <strong>lower its LUMO</strong>, shrinking the HOMO–LUMO gap. This is a "normal electron demand" Diels–Alder — the reason an electron-rich diene paired with an electron-poor dienophile reacts quickly at low temperature.</p>
</div>

<div class="callout">
<h3>The endo rule</h3>
<p><em>Endo</em> means the anhydride bridge tucks under the newly forming ring rather than pointing away (<em>exo</em>). Although the exo adduct is slightly more <strong>stable</strong>, the endo adduct forms <strong>faster</strong> because of favorable secondary orbital overlap in its transition state — a textbook case of <strong>kinetic control</strong>.</p>
</div>

## 3 · Procedure

The microscale procedure from Williamson &amp; Masters, *Macroscale and Microscale Organic Experiments* (7th ed., Cengage, Ch. 48, p. 626). Cyclopentadiene is used **freshly cracked** from its dimer (dicyclopentadiene), since it re-dimerizes on standing. Always use your instructor's version and your lab's safety rules — this is a study aid, not a substitute for a supervised SOP.

<a class="dl-btn" href="docs/diels-alder-lab-manual.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener">⬇ Download the full lab handout (PDF)</a>

| Reagent | Amount | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Maleic anhydride | 0.20 g (≈2.0 mmol) | dienophile (limiting) |
| Cyclopentadiene | 0.2 mL (≈0.16 g, ≈2.4 mmol) | diene (freshly cracked) |
| Ethyl acetate | 1 mL | solvent (dissolves maleic anhydride) |
| Hexanes | ~3 mL, cold | anti-solvent for crystallization |

1. Cool **~3 mL hexanes** in a reaction tube in an **ice bath**.
2. Weigh **0.20 g maleic anhydride** into a reaction tube or vial *(handle carefully — corrosive irritant; keep it in the hood, away from your face)*.
3. Add **1 mL ethyl acetate** to dissolve the maleic anhydride.
4. In the **fume hood**, add **0.2 mL cyclopentadiene** with stirring.
5. **Stir for 10 min** (the cycloaddition is exothermic).
6. Add **1 mL cold hexanes** dropwise with swirling; the product should crystallize.
7. **Cool for 10 min.**
8. **Decant** the excess solvent, then remove the remaining solvent from the crystals with a **Pasteur pipette**.
9. Scrape the product onto filter paper, **air-dry**, weigh, and calculate the yield.
10. Determine the **melting point** (lit. ≈165 °C); record the **IR** and **¹H NMR** spectra (handout Figs 48.8–48.9).

<div class="safety"><strong>Safety:</strong> maleic anhydride is a corrosive irritant and lachrymator — handle in the hood and avoid dust/vapor. Cyclopentadiene is volatile, flammable, and foul-smelling; keep it cold. Ethyl acetate and hexanes are flammable. Goggles and gloves. <strong>Waste:</strong> the crystallization solvent mixture goes to the organic-solvents waste; let residual solvent evaporate from the filter paper/drying agent in the hood, then place it in the nonhazardous solid waste.</div>
